# 1. Scenario Interpretation

## 1.1 Organisation and current process
### What the hub does
- Collects products from small producers: veg, gruit, eggs, preserves. 
- Prepares orders for three customer types: households, cafés, accommodation providers.
### How it works now
- Deliveries, stock, orders and collections are tracked in spreadsheets, messages and paper checklists. 
- Staff check deliveries on arrival, store them, allocate stock to orders, pack them, and hand them over at collection. 
- Shortage priority and subsitutions are decided informally, case by case.

## 1.2 Problem statement
### What's wrong
- What a producer says is coming often differs from what arrives and is accepted. (Hub manager, Producer)
- Something that has been received isn’t necessarily available, because checks may be incomplete. (Packing volunteer)
- Units vary between individual items, weight, boxes and cases, and staff convert them by hand. (“Producers, products…”)
- Customer substitution preferences aren’t recorded clearly. (Customer)
- Shortages are allocated without agreed rules. (“Orders and allocation”)
- Once stock is split across orders, the link back to producer and batch is lost. (Traceability coordinator)
### Consequences
- **Stated:** rapid reallocation is needed, orders get reopened after packing, problems are found at handover, and returned perishables can’t always go back into stock.
- **Implied:** customers get substitutes they didn’t want, hospitality customers find out about shortfalls too late (their quote suggests early notice matters), waste increases, and items can’t be traced back to their source.
